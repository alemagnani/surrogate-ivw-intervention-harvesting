\begin{block}{\textcolor{accent}{1.}\ Same item, different rank $\Rightarrow$ position bias}
Users click top results more --- partly because they are \emph{better},
partly just because they are \emph{seen} more. Under the \textbf{Position-Based
Model}, a click factorises as
\[ P(\text{click}\mid q,d,k)=\underbrace{p_k}_{\substack{\text{examination:}\\ \text{seen at rank }k}}\;\times\;\underbrace{r_{q,d}}_{\text{relevance}} . \]
The \textbf{same product}, shown at \textbf{different ranks} across groups of
logged sessions, lets us cancel relevance:
\medskip

\begin{center}
\resizebox{0.99\linewidth}{!}{\serp{1}{100}{10\%}\hspace{0.7cm}\serp{3}{40}{4\%}}
\end{center}
\medskip

\begin{center}
{\large Same query \& item $\Rightarrow$ same $r$, and $\mathrm{CTR}_k=p_k r$:}\\[0.2em]
$\displaystyle \frac{\mathrm{CTR@1}}{\mathrm{CTR@3}}=\frac{10\%}{4\%}=\frac{p_1 r}{p_3 r}=\frac{p_1}{p_3}=\textcolor{accent}{\boldsymbol{2.5{\times}}}$
\end{center}
\medskip

\hl{The CTR ratio \emph{is} the position-bias ratio} --- no A/B test, no
relevance labels. Reusing these natural rank changes is called
\emph{intervention harvesting}; the task is to combine \textbf{millions} of
such query--item cells into one estimate of $R_{k,k'}=p_k/p_{k'}$.
\smallskip

{\small\color{greytext}\textbf{Key terms:}~ \emph{cell} = one query--item pair ~$\cdot$~
$N_k$ = times shown at rank $k$ ~$\cdot$~ $p_k$ = chance rank $k$ is examined
~$\cdot$~ $\omega_i$ = that cell's weight in the average.}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{\textcolor{accent}{2.}\ Why equal weighting fails}
On the \textbf{full KDD Cup 2012} log (235.6M impressions), the cells seen at
both rank 1 and rank 2 --- \textbf{4.61M} query--item pairs, 132.3M impressions ---
have wildly uneven counts:
\medskip
\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}{0.47\linewidth}\keynum{60.7\%}{of pairs are near-empty\\($N_1\!+\!N_2\le 5$)}\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}{0.47\linewidth}\keynum{42\%}{of all impressions sit in\\the top \textbf{0.1\%} of pairs}\end{minipage}
\end{center}
\medskip
Giving a near-empty cell the \textbf{same say} as a million-impression cell
is like averaging a noisy guess and a precise measurement equally. The gain
from fixing this lives entirely in the high-count cells:
\medskip

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[y=-1.25cm]
 \foreach \lab/\val/\txt/\c [count=\i from 0] in {%
   {low-count third}/3.1/1.02$\times$/rule!35,
   {middle third}/3.3/1.09$\times$/rule!35,
   {high-count third}/15/\textbf{4.98$\times$}/accent}{
   \node[anchor=east,font=\normalsize] at (-0.3,\i) {\lab};
   \fill[\c] (0,\i-0.32) rectangle (\val,\i+0.32);
   \node[anchor=west,font=\normalsize,text=ink] at (\val+0.25,\i) {\txt};}
 \draw[dashed,thick,greytext] (3.04,2.55) -- (3.04,-0.55) node[above,font=\small,text=greytext] {$1\times$ = equal weighting};
\end{tikzpicture}\\[0.2em]
{\small\color{greytext}Efficiency vs equal weighting, by cell-count third (KDD). Efficiency $=$ variance under equal weighting $\div$ variance under harmonic; higher is better.}
\end{center}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{\textcolor{accent}{3.}\ The one-line fix: a count-only weight}
Under PBM, \emph{any} weight that depends only on counts still estimates the
same ratio $p_k/p_{k'}$ --- it changes only the noise --- so we may pick it
purely for precision. We compare three:
\medskip
\begin{center}
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{16pt}\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.75}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
\textbf{Scheme} & \textbf{Weight $\omega_i$}\\
\midrule
Equal \textit{(prior work)} & $1$\\
Min-count \textit{(new)}    & $\min(N_k^i,N_{k'}^i)$\\
\rowcolor{soft}\textbf{Harmonic} \textit{(new)} & $\dfrac{N_k^i N_{k'}^i}{N_k^i+N_{k'}^i}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\medskip
In code, per query--item pair:
\begin{center}\colorbox{ink}{\parbox{0.9\linewidth}{\centering\color{white}\large\texttt{w = n\_k * n\_kp / (n\_k + n\_kp)}}}\end{center}
\smallskip
\hl{One line.} (\texttt{n\_k}, \texttt{n\_kp} = the pair's impressions at ranks $k$, $k'$.) No experiment, no relevance labels, no tuned parameters.
\end{block}

\end{column}

\begin{block}{\textcolor{accent}{4.}\ Result: ranks 1 vs 2 (4.61M pairs, 132.3M impr.)}
\begin{center}
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{15pt}\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
Scheme & Est.\ $p_1/p_2$ & SE \footnotesize(lower better) & \textbf{Efficiency vs equal}\\
\midrule
Equal                    & 1.683 & 4.24e-3 & 1.00\\
Min                      & 1.712 & 2.05e-3 & 4.41\\
\rowcolor{soft}\textbf{Harmonic} & \textbf{1.710} & \textbf{2.02e-3} & \hl{4.53}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\medskip
\begin{center}\keynum{4.53$\times$}{equal weighting needs ${\sim}4.5\times$ the impressions for the same precision}\end{center}
\smallskip
{\small\color{greytext}\textbf{A fair caveat, read with the number:} this is \emph{precision} at a fixed
target. When PBM is imperfect, different weights average slightly different
things (equal $\to 1.68$, harmonic $\to 1.71$) --- we do \textbf{not} claim one
is the single ``true'' bias. ``Efficiency'' $=$ Var(equal)$/$Var(harmonic).}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{The surprise: the ``optimal'' oracle is \emph{worse}}
Classical theory says: weight by the exact per-cell variance, which needs the
relevance $r_i$. Plug in an \emph{estimated} $\hat r_i$ and efficiency
\textbf{collapses to} \hl{0.002} --- i.e.\ ${\sim}500\times$ \emph{worse} than equal weighting --- because
$\hat r_i$ is too noisy to be useful on the $60\%$ near-empty cells.
\smallskip

\hl{So the count-only recipe isn't a shortcut --- it is the robust choice.}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{\textcolor{accent}{5.}\ Why it works}
\large
\cmark\ Harmonic is the \textbf{inverse-variance optimum} of a count-only
approximation to the per-cell variance (the \emph{surrogate} in our title) ---
dropping the unknown relevance term is exactly what makes it \emph{deployable}.\\[0.3em]
\cmark\ \textbf{Provably no worse} than equal weighting on that surrogate variance,
for \emph{any} counts (Cauchy--Schwarz). On KDD's empirical asymmetric
surrogate it still improves $4.75\times$.\\[0.3em]
\cmark\ \textbf{No relevance estimates, no tuning} --- min-count has no such
guarantee, so we recommend harmonic.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{\textcolor{accent}{6.}\ Validation \& guardrail}
Three independent lines of evidence --- one \emph{model-based} (a variance formula applied to real data) and two \emph{ground-truth} (true variance known by simulation):
\medskip
\begin{center}
\evcard{4.5$\times$}{KDD real log}{model-based asymptotic efficiency}\hfill
\evcard{up to 54\%}{Synthetic ground truth}{43.6--54\% less variance}\hfill
\evcard{${\sim}40\%$}{Yahoo semi-synthetic}{less variance\\ (95\% CI: 29--51\% less)}
\end{center}
\medskip
Count-only weights pull further ahead of equal weighting as the log gets more
heavy-tailed --- exactly the production regime.
\medskip

{\small\color{greytext}\textbf{Guardrail.} PBM is only an approximation: across 16{,}132 high-information pairs, the per-cell bias ratios vary ${\approx}3.8\times$ more than click noise alone would explain (Cochran $Q$). So choosing a weight trades precision against \emph{which} average you estimate --- there is no single ``true'' ratio to find.}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Bottom line}
\large
Swap equal weighting for the \textbf{harmonic count-only weight}:
\textbf{one line of code}, no new data, \textbf{${\sim}4.5\times$} more
efficient on the full KDD log (ranks 1 vs 2) --- and \emph{don't} reach for the plug-in
relevance oracle, which is worse than doing nothing on heavy-tailed logs.
\end{block}
