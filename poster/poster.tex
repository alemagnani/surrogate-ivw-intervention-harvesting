\begin{block}{\textcolor{accent}{1.}\ Same item, different rank $\Rightarrow$ position bias}
Users click top results more --- partly because they are \emph{better},
partly just because they are \emph{seen} more. Under the \textbf{Position-Based
Model}, a click factorises as
\[ P(\text{click}\mid q,d,k)=\underbrace{p_k}_{\substack{\text{examination:}\\ \text{seen at rank }k}}\;\times\;\underbrace{r_{q,d}}_{\text{relevance}} . \]
The \textbf{same product X}, shown at \textbf{different ranks} across groups of
logged sessions, lets us cancel relevance:
\smallskip

\begin{center}
\resizebox{0.97\linewidth}{!}{\serp{1}{100}{10\%}\hspace{0.7cm}\serp{3}{40}{4\%}}
\end{center}
\smallskip

\begin{center}
{\large Same query \& item $\Rightarrow$ same $r$, and $\mathrm{CTR}_k=p_k r$:}\\[0.15em]
$\displaystyle \frac{\mathrm{CTR@1}}{\mathrm{CTR@3}}=\frac{10\%}{4\%}=\frac{p_1 r}{p_3 r}=\frac{p_1}{p_3}=\textcolor{accent}{\boldsymbol{2.5{\times}}}$
\end{center}
\smallskip

\hl{The CTR ratio \emph{is} the position-bias ratio} --- no A/B test, no
relevance labels. Reusing these natural rank changes is called
\emph{intervention harvesting}; the task is to combine \textbf{millions} of
such query--item cells into one estimate of $R_{k,k'}=p_k/p_{k'}$.
\smallskip

{\small\color{greytext}\textbf{Key terms:}~ \emph{cell} $i$ = one query--item pair ~$\cdot$~
$N_k^i$ = times it was shown at rank $k$ ~$\cdot$~ $C_k^i$ = clicks there ~$\cdot$~ $\omega_i$ = its weight.}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{\textcolor{accent}{2.}\ Why equal weighting fails on real logs}
On the \textbf{full KDD Cup 2012} log (235.6M impressions), the cells seen at
both rank 1 and rank 2 --- \textbf{4.61M} query--item pairs, 132.3M impressions ---
have wildly uneven counts:
\smallskip
\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}{0.47\linewidth}\keynum{60.7\%}{of pairs are near-empty\\($N_1\!+\!N_2\le 5$)}\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}{0.47\linewidth}\keynum{42\%}{of all impressions sit in\\the top \textbf{0.1\%} of pairs}\end{minipage}
\end{center}
\smallskip
Giving a near-empty cell the \textbf{same say} as a million-impression cell
averages noisy guesses and precise measurements equally. The gain
from fixing this lives in the high-count head:
\smallskip

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[y=-1.10cm]
 \foreach \lab/\val/\txt/\c [count=\i from 0] in {%
   {low-count third}/3.1/1.02$\times$/rule!35,
   {middle third}/3.3/1.09$\times$/rule!35,
   {high-count third}/15/\textbf{4.98$\times$}/accent}{
   \node[anchor=east,font=\normalsize] at (-0.3,\i) {\lab};
   \fill[\c] (0,\i-0.30) rectangle (\val,\i+0.30);
   \node[anchor=west,font=\normalsize,text=ink] at (\val+0.25,\i) {\txt};}
 \draw[dashed,thick,greytext] (3.04,2.40) -- (3.04,-0.45) node[above,font=\small,text=greytext] {$1\times$ = equal weighting};
\end{tikzpicture}\\[0.1em]
{\small\color{greytext}Efficiency ($\mathrm{Var}_{\text{equal}}/\mathrm{Var}_{\text{harmonic}}$) by cell-count tertile on KDD.\\[0.1em]
\textbf{Why not Baidu-ULTR?} In its public release $94.1\%$ of $(q,d,\mathrm{pos})$ cells have $N{=}1$ --- consistent with per-cell deduplication, unlike KDD's heavy tail.}
\end{center}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{\textcolor{accent}{3.}\ Drop-in fix: weighted ratio estimator}
Under PBM, \emph{any} count-only weight $\omega_i(N_k^i,N_{k'}^i)$ in the self-normalised ratio estimator still targets the same $p_k/p_{k'}$ --- it changes only the noise; harmonic is the one that minimises the count-only variance surrogate:
\smallskip
\begin{center}\colorbox{ink}{\parbox{0.94\linewidth}{\vspace{0.12cm}\centering\color{white}\normalsize
$\displaystyle \hat R_{k,k'} = \frac{\sum_i \omega_i\,\bigl(C_k^i / N_k^i\bigr)}{\sum_i \omega_i\,\bigl(C_{k'}^i / N_{k'}^i\bigr)}$
\quad with \quad
$\omega_i = 1 \;\longrightarrow\; \omega_i = \dfrac{N_k^i\,N_{k'}^i}{N_k^i + N_{k'}^i}$
\vspace{0.12cm}}}\end{center}
\smallskip
\begin{center}
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{12pt}\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.45}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Scheme} & \textbf{Weight $\omega_i$} & \textbf{Property}\\
\midrule
Equal \textit{(Agarwal et al.)} & $1$ & Treats $N{=}1$ and $N{=}10^6$ equally\\
Min-count \textit{(new)}    & $\min(N_k^i,N_{k'}^i)$ & Heuristic; no all-count guarantee\\
\rowcolor{soft}\textbf{Harmonic} \textit{(new)} & $\dfrac{N_k^i N_{k'}^i}{N_k^i+N_{k'}^i}$ & \textbf{Count-only surrogate IVW optimum}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\smallskip
\hl{One line of code.} Composes cleanly with \textsc{AdjacentChain}, \textsc{AllPairs}, or \textsc{Pivot} --- no labels, no tuning.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{\textcolor{accent}{5.}\ Theoretical guarantees (why harmonic?)}
\large
\cmark\ \textbf{Surrogate IVW optimum (Thm.~2):} Replacing unknown cell relevances $r_i$ with a common factor yields the count-only variance surrogate $V_k(\omega)+V_{k'}(\omega)$, which harmonic minimises.\\[0.2em]
\cmark\ \textbf{Unconditional Cauchy--Schwarz bound (Thm.~3):} $V_k(\omega^h)+V_{k'}(\omega^h)\le V_k(\mathbf 1)+V_{k'}(\mathbf 1)$ for \emph{any} counts $\{N_k^i,N_{k'}^i\}$ (min-count has no such guarantee). On KDD's asymmetric surrogate $\alpha V_k+\beta V_{k'}$, harmonic still improves \textbf{4.75$\times$}.\\[0.2em]
\cmark\ \textbf{Why not pair-bootstrap?} Resampling $(q,d)$ pairs perturbs the few high-information cells and entangles efficiency with model fit; the closed-form (delta-method) $\widetilde V$ isolates conditional efficiency.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{\textcolor{accent}{6.}\ Ground-truth validation \& PBM guardrail}
Independent ground-truth simulations confirm the delta-method efficiency gains and test robustness under explicit click-model violations:
\smallskip
\begin{center}
\evcard{up to 54\%}{Synthetic PBM}{less variance vs equal;\\up to \textbf{2$\times$ data savings}}\hfill
\evcard{${\sim}40\%$}{Yahoo-LTR}{less variance (CI: 29--51\%);\\clipping inflates MSE \textbf{458$\times$}}\hfill
\evcard{3 / 3}{PBM Violations}{lower MSE than \textbf{Equal} under\\\textbf{Trust}, \textbf{Cascade} \& \textbf{Pos-Dep}}
\end{center}
\smallskip
{\small\color{greytext}\textbf{Guardrail (Cochran's $Q$).} Across 16{,}132 high-information KDD pairs ($\ge 5$ clicks/rank), per-cell odds ratios vary \textbf{$3.83\times$} more than binomial click noise ($Q/\mathrm{df}=3.83$), confirming PBM is an approximation and weight choice is a precision vs.\ estimand trade-off.}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Bottom line}
\large
Replace $\omega_i=1$ with \textbf{$\omega_i = N_k^i N_{k'}^i / (N_k^i + N_{k'}^i)$}:
\textbf{one line of code}, no extra data, \textbf{${\sim}4.5\times$} higher efficiency on full KDD (ranks 1 vs 2), and lower MSE than equal weighting under all three tested PBM violations --- no relevance labels, no tuning.
\end{block}
